# Erdős #1142 — multiplicative-order subgroup sieve

**Author:** Jared Wilder  
**Recovered from:** September 2026 theorem-repair / fusion audit  
**Public extraction:** 2026-09-11

Define `Good(n)` to mean that

\[
n-2^j
\]

is prime for every power `2^j` satisfying

\[
1<2^j<n.
\]

This packet records the exact modular sieve recovered from the campaign. Stronger 2026 public
computations already use related primitive-root machinery much farther, so the result is released as
verified infrastructure rather than a novelty headline.

## Orbit-avoidance theorem

Let `p` be an odd prime and let

\[
r=\operatorname{ord}_p(2).
\]

If

\[
n>p+2^r
\]

and `Good(n)` holds, then

\[
\boxed{n\bmod p\notin\langle2\rangle}
\]

unless `p|n`. Equivalently, a nonzero residue of `n mod p` cannot lie in the subgroup generated by
2.

### Proof

Assume `p∤n` and `n mod p∈<2>`. Choose `j` with `0<=j<r` and

\[
n\equiv2^j\pmod p.
\]

If `j=0`, replace it by `j=r`, since `2^r≡1 mod p`. Thus in every case choose

\[
1\le j\le r,
\qquad n\equiv2^j\pmod p.
\]

The threshold gives

\[
n-2^j\ge n-2^r>p>1.
\]

But `p|(n-2^j)`, so `n-2^j` is a multiple of `p` strictly larger than `p`; it is composite. Since
`1<2^j<n`, this contradicts `Good(n)`.

## Primitive-root corollary

If 2 is a primitive root modulo `p`, then `<2>` is the entire nonzero residue group. Hence, past the
same threshold,

\[
\boxed{Good(n)\Longrightarrow p\mid n.}
\]

For `p=3`, `ord_3(2)=2`; for `p=5`, `ord_5(2)=4`. Therefore if `Good(n)` and `n>21`, then

\[
\boxed{15\mid n.}
\]

The strict threshold matters: the campaign retained `n=21,p=5` as the regression counterexample to
an earlier overstrong boundary statement.

## Simultaneous CRT compression

For a fixed order cutoff `D`, once `n` is above all relevant thresholds, impose the orbit-avoidance
condition simultaneously for every odd prime with

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\le D.
\]

The late estate's `D=43` fusion produced a large exact CRT compression and, in its primitive-root
forcing component, the recorded divisor

\[
43,730,115
\]

for sufficiently large candidates (`n>2^44` in the source package). That finite fused computation is
preserved in the mine ledger; the universal mathematical kernel is the orbit-avoidance theorem above.
