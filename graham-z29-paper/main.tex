\begin{abstract}
We give a reproducible computational certificate establishing the Graham/Alspach sequenceability conjecture for the cyclic group $\mathbb Z_{29}$. A recent general theorem \cite{costa2026} covers all subsets of cardinality at most $20$. For every subset cardinality from $21$ through $28$, we quotient by the action of $\mathbb Z_{29}^{\times}$, produce a sequencing witness for each of $60{,}134$ canonical orbit representatives, and independently verify exact coverage of all $1{,}683{,}218$ target subsets. Two independently implemented verifiers use different coverage arguments: full orbit expansion into a $2^{28}$-element bit universe, and canonical-orbit accounting by stabilizer size. The certificate is deterministic across thread counts and survives adversarial mutation tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}
For a finite subset $A$ of an abelian group, call an ordering $a_1,\dots,a_m$ a sequencing when the partial sums $s_i=\sum_{j=1}^i a_j$ are pairwise distinct and $s_i\ne0$ for $1\le i<m$.

\begin{theorem}
Every subset $A\subseteq\mathbb Z_{29}\setminus\{0\}$ is sequenceable.
\end{theorem}

\section{Reduction by symmetry}
Let $U=\mathbb Z_{29}^{\times}$ act on subsets by $u\cdot A=\{ua:a\in A\}$.

\begin{lemma}
If $(a_1,\dots,a_m)$ sequences $A$, then $(ua_1,\dots,ua_m)$ sequences $uA$ for every $u\in U$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every partial sum is multiplied by the nonzero scalar $u$. Thus equality or vanishing of scaled partial sums is equivalent to equality or vanishing before scaling.
\end{proof}

It therefore suffices to exhibit one sequencing for each orbit. We encode a subset of $\{1,\dots,28\}$ by a $28$-bit mask and choose the numerically least mask in each orbit as its canonical representative.

\section{Independent verification}
The Go verifier parses the certificate independently, validates each representative ordering, scales each witness by all $28$ units, and marks the resulting subsets in a bitset indexed by all $28$-bit masks. It checks exact cardinality counts and reports $1{,}683{,}218$ unique covered subsets after $1{,}683{,}752$ scaled-witness checks.

The Python verifier independently recomputes each canonical orbit and its stabilizer, validates every representative witness, rejects duplicate canonical representatives, and sums the exact disjoint orbit sizes. For each cardinality $k$, the sum equals $\binom{28}{k}$.

The two methods agree on every cardinality, the total, the representative count, the orbit-size histogram, and the certificate digest. Both reject certificates with a missing representative, a duplicated orbit, a corrupted ordering, or a false total field.

\section{Proof of the theorem}
For $|A|\le20$, invoke the published general small-set theorem \cite{costa2026}. For $21\le|A|\le28$, exact coverage verification places $A$ in the orbit of a unique canonical representative $R$ in the certificate. The certificate supplies a verified sequencing of $R$; the symmetry lemma scales it to a sequencing of $A$. These ranges exhaust all subsets of $\mathbb Z_{29}\setminus\{0\}$.

\section{Reproducibility and scope}
The witness generator is not trusted as a verifier. A second generation with a different thread count produced the identical byte-level certificate. The complete sources, certificate, verification reports, and mutation tests accompany this manuscript. The mathematical claim is computationally verified; external peer review and priority adjudication are not asserted here.
