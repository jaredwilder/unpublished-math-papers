# Dynamic Desnanot harmonicity for positive determinant homotopies

**Author:** Jared Wilder  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** exact generic determinant theorem; **not an RH proof**  
**Novelty:** targeted search did not locate this exact formulation; historical priority remains **unverified**

## 1. The theorem is more general than the RH archive stated

The research archive originally introduced the following identities along the de Bruijn–Newman coefficient flow. The heat equation is **not actually needed for the identities themselves**.

Let

\[
D_{r,k}(t)>0
\]

be any `C^2` one-parameter family of positive consecutive-determinant arrays on a lattice domain, with each time slice satisfying the Desnanot–Jacobi normalized identity

\[
R^D_{r,k}(t)+A^D_{r,k}(t)=1,
\tag{1}
\]

where

\[
R^D_{r,k}
:=
\frac{D_{r,k-1}D_{r,k+1}}{D_{r,k}^2},
\qquad
A^D_{r,k}
:=
\frac{D_{r-1,k}D_{r+1,k}}{D_{r,k}^2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let `B_(r,k)>0` be any **time-independent** positive comparison array satisfying the same normalized Desnanot identity, and define

\[
U_{r,k}(t)
:=
\log\frac{D_{r,k}(t)}{B_{r,k}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Write

\[
\Delta_k^2f_{r,k}
=f_{r,k-1}-2f_{r,k}+f_{r,k+1},
\]

and similarly for `Delta_r^2`.

Then the exact normalized comparison equation is

\[
R^B_{r,k}
 e^{\Delta_k^2U_{r,k}}
+
A^B_{r,k}
 e^{\Delta_r^2U_{r,k}}
=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Moreover,

\[
R^D
=R^B e^{\Delta_k^2U},
\qquad
A^D
=A^B e^{\Delta_r^2U}.
\tag{5}
\]

## 2. First derivative: adaptive harmonicity

Set

\[
V_{r,k}:=\partial_tU_{r,k}.
\]

Differentiate (4). Since `B` is fixed in time,

\[
\boxed{
R^D_{r,k}\Delta_k^2V_{r,k}
+
A^D_{r,k}\Delta_r^2V_{r,k}
=0.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Because

\[
R^D>0,
\qquad
A^D>0,
\qquad
R^D+A^D=1,
\]

`V` is harmonic for the **state-dependent nearest-neighbor elliptic operator**

\[
L_Df
:=R^D\Delta_k^2f+A^D\Delta_r^2f.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the logarithmic velocity of the determinant ratio is not merely governed by a linearization around a benchmark. It is exactly harmonic in the adaptive metric generated by the current determinant array itself.

### Finite-domain consequence

On a finite connected lattice domain, `V` obeys the usual weighted discrete maximum/minimum principle: absent a constant plateau, a strict extremum cannot first occur in the interior.

## 3. Second derivative: adaptive superharmonicity

Set

\[
W_{r,k}:=\partial_t^2U_{r,k}.
\]

Differentiate (6), or differentiate (4) twice. Since

\[
\partial_tR^D
=R^D\Delta_k^2V,
\qquad
\partial_tA^D
=A^D\Delta_r^2V,
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
R^D\Delta_k^2W
+
A^D\Delta_r^2W
=
-R^D(\Delta_k^2V)^2
-A^D(\Delta_r^2V)^2
\le0.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Thus the logarithmic acceleration is **superharmonic in the same adaptive determinant metric**, with an explicit negative quadratic source.

The source vanishes exactly when

\[
\Delta_k^2V=0,
\qquad
\Delta_r^2V=0,
\tag{9}
\]

at that lattice point.

### Finite-domain consequence

With the sign convention in (7), an interior strict minimum of `W` is incompatible with (8) unless the relevant second differences and source degenerate. Equivalently, lower bounds for the logarithmic acceleration propagate from the boundary through a finite positive domain.

## 4. Proof in one line per derivative

Equation (4) is the normalized Desnanot identity for `D/B`.

Differentiating once gives

\[
R^B e^{\Delta_k^2U}\Delta_k^2V
+
A^B e^{\Delta_r^2U}\Delta_r^2V=0,
\]

which becomes (6) by (5).

Differentiating again gives

\[
R^D\bigl[(\Delta_k^2V)^2+\Delta_k^2W\bigr]
+
A^D\bigl[(\Delta_r^2V)^2+\Delta_r^2W\bigr]
=0,
\]

which rearranges to (8).

No heat equation has been used.

## 5. de Bruijn–Newman application

For the Riemann Xi heat deformation, the coefficient generating function satisfies

\[
\partial_t\mathcal G_t
=4z\,\partial_z^2\mathcal G_t
+2\,\partial_z\mathcal G_t,
\]

and therefore supplies a canonical determinant homotopy `D_(r,k)(t)`.

As long as the neighboring consecutive minors remain positive, (6)–(8) apply exactly. In that application,

\[
U_{r,k}(t)=\log\frac{D_{r,k}(t)}{B_{r,k}}
\]

may be taken relative to the factorial benchmark or another fixed positive Desnanot comparison array.

The identities do **not** prove that the required positivity region persists globally, nor do they control the escape-to-infinity boundary by themselves. Those are separate analytic obligations.

## 6. Relation to the static comparison theorem

The static normalized equation

\[
R^B e^{\Delta_k^2U}
+A^B e^{\Delta_r^2U}=1
\]

has a finite-domain comparison principle because both exponential terms are strictly increasing in their second differences.

The present theorem adds a dynamic layer:

- `U` solves a nonlinear discrete elliptic equation;
- `partial_t U` solves the exact linearized equation in the **current** adaptive metric;
- `partial_t^2 U` solves the same operator with a nonpositive quadratic forcing term.

This is the determinant-lattice analogue of differentiating a nonlinear elliptic family and obtaining a Jacobi-field equation plus a signed second-variation source.

## 7. Prior-art boundary

Desnanot–Jacobi condensation, Toda-type determinant dynamics, discrete elliptic maximum principles and related integrable systems are classical subjects.

A targeted search for the exact package

\[
L_D(\partial_t\log(D/B))=0,
\qquad
L_D(\partial_t^2\log(D/B))
=-\text{quadratic form}
\]

for positive Desnanot determinant homotopies did not locate an exact published counterpart. That negative search is **not** proof of novelty. Because the derivation is short once the normalized potential is chosen, historical priority should be reviewed by a specialist in determinant condensation / integrable systems before any novelty claim is made.

## 8. What is not claimed

This note does **not** claim:

- RH;
- positivity of any specific determinant family outside the assumed positive domain;
- a global Phragmen–Lindelof or no-escape theorem;
- reversibility of the adaptive random walk;
- that the de Bruijn–Newman PDE is required for the generic theorem;
- settled literature novelty.

It records an exact dynamic law for arbitrary positive determinant homotopies and identifies the de Bruijn–Newman coefficient flow as one important application.
