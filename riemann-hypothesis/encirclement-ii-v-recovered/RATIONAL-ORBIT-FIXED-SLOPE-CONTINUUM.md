# Rational-orbit fixed-slope continuum and linearization

**Author:** Jared Wilder  
**Recovered from:** RH Encirclement II

Encirclement II corrected an earlier scaling mistake and derived the continuum law around the exact rational odds orbit.

## 1. Scaling correction

For the Riemann first-row curvature write

\[
R_{1,k}=e^{-\tau_k},
\qquad
Y_{1,k}=e^{\tau_k}-1.
\]

The available curvature window gives

\[
\tau_k\asymp k^{-1},
\]

so in the small-curvature regime

\[
Y_{r,k}\approx r\tau_k\asymp\frac rk.
\]

Hence

\[
k\asymp r^3\Rightarrow Y\asymp r^{-2},
\]

\[
k\asymp r^2\Rightarrow Y\asymp r^{-1},
\]

and

\[
k\asymp r\Rightarrow Y=O(1).
\]

Thus an ansatz of the form `Y=r^-1 y(k/r)+...` belongs to the **quadratic transition** `k~r^2`, not to fixed slope.

---

## 2. Correct fixed-slope ansatz

Set

\[
\alpha=\frac{k}{r}
\]

and use

\[
\boxed{
Y_{r,k}
=
y(\alpha)+\frac1r z(\alpha)+O(r^{-2}).
}
\]

Expanding the exact nonlinear odds recurrence gives, at first nontrivial order,

\[
\boxed{
(\alpha^2y+1)p''
+2\alpha y\,p'
+\frac{y(\alpha^2-1)}{y+1}(p')^2
=0,
\qquad p=\log y.
}
\]

The exact solution selected by the factorial benchmark is

\[
\boxed{y(\alpha)=\frac1\alpha.}
\]

---

## 3. Next correction

Substituting `y=1/alpha` into the next-order equation gives

\[
\boxed{
\alpha^2z''+4\alpha z'+2z=0.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
z(\alpha)=\frac A\alpha+\frac B{\alpha^2}.}
\]

This correction family is exactly tangent to the discrete two-parameter rational orbit

\[
\frac{r+\mu}{k+\nu}
=
\frac1\alpha
+
\frac1r
\left(
\frac\mu\alpha-
\frac\nu{\alpha^2}
\right)
+O(r^{-2}).
\]

So the continuum expansion does not merely approximate the rational family; its first correction directions are precisely the tangent directions generated by shifts in the exact discrete orbit.

---

## 4. Exact normalized map

Normalize the odds by the unshifted exact orbit:

\[
\boxed{
Z^{\rm II}_{r,k}=\frac{k}{r}Y_{r,k}.
}
\]

The factorial benchmark becomes

\[
\boxed{Z^{\rm II}_{r,k}\equiv1.}
\]

Writing

\[
B_{r,k}=\frac rk,
\]

the exact normalized recurrence is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1+B_{r+1,k}Z_{r+1,k}}
{1+B_{r+1,k}}
=
Z_{r,k}^2
\frac{1+B_{r-1,k}}
{1+B_{r-1,k}Z_{r-1,k}}
\prod_{\sigma=\pm1}
\frac{1+B_{r,k+\sigma}Z_{r,k+\sigma}}
{(1+B_{r,k+\sigma})Z_{r,k+\sigma}}.
}
\]

The constant field `Z=1` is an exact fixed solution.

---

## 5. Finite-lattice Jacobian

Linearize around the orbit by

\[
Z_{r,k}=1+\varepsilon_{r,k}.
\]

The exact one-step Jacobian is

\[
\frac{\partial Z_{r+1,k}}{\partial Z_{r-1,k}}
=-\frac{(r-1)(k+r+1)}{(r+1)(k+r-1)},
\]

\[
\frac{\partial Z_{r+1,k}}{\partial Z_{r,k-1}}
=-\frac{(k-1)(k+r+1)}{(r+1)(k+r-1)},
\]

\[
\frac{\partial Z_{r+1,k}}{\partial Z_{r,k}}
=\frac{2(k+r+1)}{r+1},
\]

and

\[
\frac{\partial Z_{r+1,k}}{\partial Z_{r,k+1}}
=-\frac{k+1}{r+1}.
\]

## 6. Frozen fixed-slope dispersion

At

\[
k=\alpha r,
\qquad r\to\infty,
\]

the linearized recurrence becomes

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{r+1,k}
=
-\varepsilon_{r-1,k}
-\alpha\varepsilon_{r,k-1}
+2(\alpha+1)\varepsilon_{r,k}
-\alpha\varepsilon_{r,k+1}
+o(1).
}
\]

For a local Fourier mode

\[
\varepsilon\sim\lambda^r e^{i\theta k},
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
\lambda+\lambda^{-1}
=
2+4\alpha\sin^2(\theta/2).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\omega(\theta;\alpha)
=
2\operatorname{arsinh}
\left(\sqrt\alpha\,|\sin(\theta/2)|\right),
\qquad
\lambda_\pm=e^{\pm\omega}.
}
\]

For the longest wavelengths on an `O(r)` fixed-slope window,

\[
\theta\asymp r^{-1},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{r\omega=O(1).}
\]

Thus normalization by the exact rational orbit removes the spurious long-wave `exp(c sqrt(r))` instability produced by the earlier mis-scaled constant-background analysis.

High-frequency modes remain unstable, so a complete invariant-tube theorem still requires additional spatial regularity control.

## 7. Riemann first-row position relative to the orbit

The first-row curvature satisfies

\[
R_{1,k}=e^{-\tau_k},
\qquad
Y_{1,k}=e^{\tau_k}-1.
\]

The strict Turán side of the available curvature window gives

\[
R_{1,k}<\frac{k}{k+1},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{Y_{1,k}>\frac1k.}
\]

Therefore the Encirclement-II normalized seed obeys

\[
\boxed{Z^{\rm II}_{1,k}=kY_{1,k}>1.}
\]

The upper curvature bound gives

\[
1<Z^{\rm II}_{1,k}<k(e^{4/k}-1).
\]

A componentwise comparison principle does not follow because the exact recurrence has negative neighboring-variable derivatives. The seed location is exact information, not by itself a propagation theorem.
